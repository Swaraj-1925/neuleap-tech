
## ETL Pipeline Overview (load_data Function)

**Purpose**
* Normalizes ID fields that may appear as floating-point values
* Drops invalid or logically impossible rows
* Standardizes date formats
* Prevents foreign key constraint violations by filtering:

  * Orders referencing non-existent customers
  * Order items referencing missing orders or products
  * Payments referencing missing orders


---

## Query 1: Top 5 Highest Value Completed Orders

**Purpose**
Identifies the five most expensive completed orders by computing the total order value after applying discounts and taxes.

**Logic**

* Joins `orders`, `order_items`, and `products`
* Calculates order value as:
  `(sum of quantity × product price) − discount + tax`
* Filters only orders with status `Completed`
* Orders results by total value in descending order
* Limits output to top five orders

**Result Interpretation**
The output shows the highest-value completed orders, with order amounts ranging from approximately 4707 to 4973. These orders represent the most financially significant transactions in the system.

---

## Query 2: Customers with More Than 2 Orders

**Purpose**
Identifies repeat customers who have placed more than two orders.

**Logic**

* Joins `customers` with `orders`
* Groups by customer
* Counts the number of orders per customer
* Filters groups having more than two orders using `HAVING`

**Result Interpretation**
The empty result indicates that no customer in the dataset has placed more than two orders, suggesting a low repeat purchase rate or a dataset dominated by one-time buyers.

---

## Query 3: Customers Who Never Placed an Order

**Purpose**
Finds customers who registered but never made a purchase.

**Logic**

* Performs a `LEFT JOIN` between `customers` and `orders`
* Filters rows where `order_id IS NULL`

**Result Interpretation**
The empty result indicates that every customer in the database has placed at least one order.

---

## Query 4: Most Expensive Product Purchased by Each Customer

**Purpose**
Identifies the highest-priced product purchased by each customer.

**Logic**

* Joins `orders`, `order_items`, and `products`
* Uses `DISTINCT ON (customer_id)`
* Orders products per customer by price in descending order
* Selects the top-priced product per customer

**Result Interpretation**
The result contains one row per customer (999 rows), showing the most expensive product each customer has purchased.

---

## Query 5: Order with the Largest Number of Items

**Purpose**
Finds the order containing the highest total quantity of items.

**Logic**

* Aggregates `quantity` from `order_items` by order
* Orders results by total quantity in descending order
* Limits output to the top order

**Result Interpretation**
Order ID 70 contains the highest number of items, with a total quantity of 5.

## Query 6: Customers Who Ordered Within 30 Days of Signup

**Purpose**
Identifies customers who placed their first order within 30 days of signup.

**Logic**

* Joins `customers` and `orders`
* Uses `MIN(order_date)` to identify first purchase
* Computes the date difference between signup and first order
* Filters customers with a difference of 30 days or less

**Result Interpretation**
257 customers placed their first order within 30 days of signup. Negative date differences indicate data inconsistencies where order dates precede signup dates.

## Query 8: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

**Purpose**
Calculates total revenue generated by each customer.

**Logic**

* Joins `customers`, `orders`, `order_items`, and `products`
* Aggregates revenue as `quantity × price`
* Groups by customer
* Orders results by total revenue

**Result Interpretation**
All customers generate similar revenue values, indicating low variance in customer spending.

## Query 15: Net Revenue After Discounts and Tax (Monthly, Country-wise)

**Purpose**
Calculates net revenue by month and country.

**Logic**

* Computes gross revenue from item-level sales
* Aggregates discounts and taxes
* Calculates net revenue as:
  `gross − discount + tax`
* Groups by country, year, and month

**Result Interpretation**
All countries show peak net revenue in October and November 2025, with consistent discount and tax impact.

## Query 16: Discount Sensitivity

**Purpose**
Identifies customers who only place discounted orders.

**Logic**

* Counts total orders per customer
* Counts discounted orders per customer
* Filters customers where both counts are equal

**Result Interpretation**
All customers appear discount-sensitive due to every customer having exactly one discounted order, making the result data-driven rather than behavioral.

## Query 17: First vs Most Recent Purchase Product

**Purpose**
Compares the first and most recent purchased product per customer.

**Logic**

* Selects first and last ranked products per customer

**Result Interpretation**
First and last products are identical for all customers due to single-order behavior.

## Query 18: Pareto Revenue Analysis (80/20 Rule)

**Purpose**
Identifies customers contributing to approximately 80% of total revenue.

**Logic**

* Calculates total revenue per customer
* Orders customers by revenue descending
* Computes cumulative and total revenue
* Filters customers until cumulative revenue reaches 80%

**Result Interpretation**
514 customers contribute to approximately 80% of total revenue, indicating a relatively even revenue distribution.

---
